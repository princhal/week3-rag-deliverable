Three published results shape the whole decision. First, context loss hurts more than boundary placement:       Anthropic's contextual retrieval work found that prepending a short generated description of where a chunk sits in       its document cut top-20 retrieval failures by 35% on its own, 49% with contextual BM25, and 67% with reranking       added. Second, clever boundaries are oversold: the NAACL 2025 study by Qu, Tu and Bao found that semantic       chunking's computational cost is not justified by consistent gains over plain fixed-size splitting. Third, ordering       beats splitting: late chunking — encoding the whole document first and pooling per-chunk vectors afterwards —       improved retrieval across every boundary strategy it was paired with, which means it is a layer on top of your                                                                                          boundary choice rather than a replacement for it.                                                                                                                      THE UNCOMFORTABLE IMPLICATION                                                      If you are choosing between semantic chunking and recursive splitting before you have added hybrid search, a reranker, or        document context to your chunks, you are optimising the parameter with the smallest published effect size. Do the cheap        high-yield work first, then tune boundaries against your own eval set.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   The one rule that survives every corpus                                            ▪ A chunk must be interpretable alone. If a human analyst handed that chunk on an index card could not tell you what        document, section and entity it refers to, the embedding cannot either.           ▪ A chunk must be smaller than the answer's blast radius. Retrieval precision collapses when one vector has to represent five        unrelated ideas.                                                                  ▪ A chunk must be reconstructable. Stable IDs, character offsets and a section path, or you can never cite it, diff it or re-        index it safely. Configuration A fails on boundary — the exception was severed from the right it modifies, and no reranker can       repair a vector that never encoded the subject. Three stacks that cover most builds                                                                                                                                   The starter stack — recursive boundaries at 500 tokens with 12% overlap, hybrid dense and BM25 retrieval,       reranking over the top 20. Build this first regardless of corpus; it is the baseline every later change is measured       against. The document stack — structure-aware boundaries on the native seam, parent-document assembly,       contextual prefixes carrying entity and version. This is the right shape for contracts, filings, manuals and technical       documentation, and it is where most enterprise deployments should end up. The high-stakes stack — the       document stack plus proposition rewriting and a hierarchical summary level, justified only when the corpus is small       enough to re-chunk on demand and a wrong retrieval carries real consequences.       What you should not do is assemble a fourth stack out of whichever techniques were most recently written about.                                                                                          Every layer added here costs index time, query latency, or both, and each one needs a measured reason to exist —       which is what Section 07 is for.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   SECTION 03                                                                         The  five-question    decision   path                                                                                                                                 Run these in order. Each question eliminates options; by question five you have a boundary policy, a size band       and an assembly layer. Stop at the first question that gives a decisive answer for your corpus.                                                                                           Q1 · STRUCTURE Does the document carry structure you can trust?                               Not "does it have headings somewhere" — does every document in the corpus have them, parsed                   reliably, at a usable granularity? Test on 20 random documents before committing.                                                                                                      YES → Structure-aware split on the native seam (heading, clause, function, turn), with a size cap                   that sub-splits oversized sections recursively.                                    NO → Recursive splitting. Consider semantic boundaries only if you will measure them.                                          ↓                                                Q2 · ANSWER Is the answer a span, a section, or a synthesis?                       SHAPE                                                                                         Pull 30 real questions from your users or support logs and mark where the answer physically lives. This                   single exercise resolves most chunk-size debates without a benchmark.                   SPAN (a sentence or two) → Small chunks, 200–400 tokens, plus parent-document assembly.                   SECTION → Structure-aware chunks at 600–1,000 tokens, returned whole.                   SYNTHESIS / MULTI-HOP → Add a hierarchical summary level; chunk size alone will not fix this.                                          ↓                                                Q3 ·       Can a chunk be understood without its neighbours?                       INDEPENDENCE                                                                                  Print ten random chunks. If they open with "it", "this section", "the above requirement", or a bare                   number whose subject sits three pages earlier, your embeddings are encoding ambiguity.                   NO → Add contextual retrieval (generated prefix + contextual BM25), or late chunking if your                   embedding provider supports it. Both, if the corpus justifies it.                   YES → Skip the augmentation layer and spend the budget on reranking instead.                                          ↓                                                Q4 · BUDGET & What does one document cost to index, and how often does it change?        CHURN                                                                                         Multiply your per-document strategy cost by corpus size, then again by expected re-index cycles per                   year. Any LLM-in-the-loop strategy must survive that second multiplication.                   HIGH CHURN / LARGE CORPUS → Deterministic boundaries only; keep LLM passes for a curated high-                   value subset.                                                                      STATIC / SMALL → LLM or proposition chunking is affordable and usually wins on quality.                                          ↓                                                Q5 · CITATION & Do you have to cite, audit or restrict what was retrieved?         ACL                                                                                           Regulated domains make this non-negotiable, and it constrains boundaries: a chunk that spans two                   clauses or two tenants cannot be cited or filtered cleanly.                        YES → Never cross a structural or permission boundary in a chunk. Carry doc_id, section path,                   page, char offsets, version and ACL scope on every chunk. Prefer smaller chunks with parent                   expansion over large merged ones.                                                  NO → Optimise purely for recall and cost.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   SECTION 05                                                                         Size, overlap   and  the  metadata    that  must  ride  along                                                                                                         Once the strategy is chosen, three parameters remain. Two of them are widely over-thought; the third — the       chunk envelope — is widely ignored and causes most production pain.                                                                                                   Chunk size                                                                                                                                                            Size is bounded from below by the length of a typical answer span and from above by your embedding model's       maximum sequence length. Anything longer is silently truncated by most encoders — the API returns a vector, no       error is raised, and the tail of your chunk is simply not represented. Verify the limit for your specific model and       count tokens with that model's tokenizer, not a character heuristic and not a different vendor's. Below 5% you sever       answers; above 25% you are paying to store and rerank near-duplicates, and your top-k fills with three copies of                                                                                          the same passage — which is worse than it sounds, because it crowds out the genuinely different chunk that held       the rest of the answer. If you have adopted structure-aware boundaries, set overlap to zero: the boundary is       already meaningful, and overlap only blurs it. Deduplicate at retrieval time regardless.                                                                                          The chunk envelope                                                                 Every chunk should be stored with the fields below. This is not bookkeeping — it is what makes citation, permission       filtering, incremental re-indexing and A/B comparison possible. Retrofitting it after launch means re-indexing the       whole corpus.                                                                                                                                                          {                                                                                   "chunk_id": "sha256(doc_id + version + char_start)",                               "doc_id": "stable across versions",                                                "doc_version": "content hash or CMS revision",                                     "section_path": ["Part II", "Clause 7", "7.3 Termination"],                        "page":   14,                                                                      "char_start": 18422,                                                               "char_end": 19105,                                                                 "parent_id": "id of the section chunk to expand to",                               "acl_scope": ["tenant:acme", "role:legal"],                                        "source_type": "contract | code | ticket | transcript",                            "chunk_config": "recursive-v3:800:0.12",                                           "indexed_at": "06/09/2026T11:20:00+05:30"                                         }                                                                                 ▪ chunk_config is the field teams forget. Stamping the strategy and parameters on every chunk is what lets you run two        configurations side by side, compare them honestly, and roll back a bad re-chunk without rebuilding the index. Size is a property of the pair, not the corpus                                     The optimal chunk size belongs to the combination of your corpus and your embedding model, which is why       borrowed numbers travel so badly. A model trained on short retrieval passages behaves differently at 900 tokens       than one built for long documents, and models differ in how gracefully quality degrades as a chunk approaches       their sequence limit. Treat every embedding model swap as an invalidation event: the same eval set, re-run, before                                                                                          the migration is called done.                                                                                                                                          THREE ASSERTIONS WORTH ADDING TO THE INGEST JOB                                    One: every chunk's token count, measured with the embedding model's own tokenizer, is under the model limit — fail the        batch, do not silently truncate. Two: no chunk is under about 50 tokens; fragments that short are usually orphaned        headings or table debris that pollute the top-k. Three: every chunk carries a non-null parent_id and section_path, so        nothing enters the index that cannot be expanded or cited later.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   CLOSING                                                                            The  honest   summary                                                                                                                                                 There is no best chunking strategy, and anyone offering you one has not seen your corpus.                                                                                          There is, however, a defensible one. Match the boundary to the document's own structure. Match the size to the       shape of the answers your users actually need. Add document context to chunks that cannot stand alone, and only       to those. Keep the retrieval improvements — hybrid search and reranking — ahead of the chunking improvements in       your queue, because that is the order the evidence supports. And hold on to an evaluation set that lets you change       your mind cheaply when the corpus grows, the model changes, or the questions your users ask turn out to be       different from the ones you designed for.                                                                                                                             The teams that get this right are not the ones with the most sophisticated splitter. They are the ones who can       answer, in one sentence and with a number, why their chunks are the size they are.
